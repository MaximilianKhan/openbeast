The Spare-Memory Meta

The state of weight compression for VRAM-poor, compute-rich GPUs — where the field moved between August 11 and September 8, where our campaign sits inside it, and every paper we processed to know that.

1Why this research exists

Consumer GPU economics produced a strange machine: the RTX 5090 carries formidable compute strapped to a 32 GB VRAM pool too small for frontier models. Our measurements sharpened what that means — batch-1 decode is memory-bandwidth-bound, so on our own card a 27B at Q6 decodes at 61 tok/s while the same model at Q2_K runs 99.7. Smaller weights aren't a compromise that costs speed; they're faster. The binding trade was never compute-for-memory. It is accuracy-for-memory, with compute as the free resource that buys accuracy back.

beast-rank is our campaign to exploit that: store quantized or factorized weights, and spend the idle compute reconstructing accuracy at inference — low-rank corrections fused into the serving path of llama.cpp, applied to the exact GGUF artifacts people actually download. Twenty-nine experiments in, we hold a set of measured results and a paper draft; this briefing is the map of everyone else's territory around them.

The framing is still ours. The closest published neighbors to the "compute idles — spend it on accuracy reconstruction" inversion are CPU-side (cflow, 2608.23841) or hardware-community diagnoses that decode is memory-bound (Ma & Patterson, 2601.05047). Nobody has published the GPU-side version. Independent corroboration keeps arriving: Blackwell's FP4 tensor cores gave llama.cpp +68% prefill and ~0% decode — compute got faster and the token rate didn't move, because bytes-in-VRAM is the wall.

2The five fronts of the field

Weight-compression research in late 2026 is best read as five fronts. Every paper we processed belongs to one.

Front A

Produce-time PTQ

Make a better quantized artifact from the original weights: GPTQ lineage, rotation methods, grouping tricks (OCGQuant), Schur-complement optimizers (SchurQuant), E2E gradient PTQ (REAL-Q). Crowded, fast-moving, vendor-heavy.

Front B

Shipped-artifact repair

Improve the quantized file people already have, ideally in-format. GSQ and ReQuant opened this in April–August; our gguf-refine is the one-shot whitened instantiation. Three groups worldwide, one of them us.

Front C

Sidecar correction

Keep the base frozen; add a small correction structure — low-rank adapters (ours, LQER lineage), seeded random bases (AWSRC), quantized full residuals (RRQ), vector codebooks. The battle is capture-per-byte.

Front D

Hardware formats

NVFP4/MXFP4 native weights riding Blackwell tensor cores. NVIDIA ships production checkpoints (DeepSeek-V4-NVFP4); Moonshot trains K3 natively in MXFP4; the toolchains still ship zero low-rank correction.

Front E

Serving systems

Where compression meets the engine: llama.cpp's quant kernels, speculative/MTP paths, adapter fusion. SVDQuant/Nunchaku proved fused low-rank + 4-bit serving — for diffusion models only. The LLM slot is open.

4What moved in the window — the eight facts that matter

4.1 · Muon may be inside our own model

Qwen's architecture paper for Qwen3.8-Next (2608.30320) discloses that the model was trained with the Muon optimizer on all genuine 2-D linear maps — attention, GDN, expert weights — with AdamW kept for embeddings and routers. Third-party coverage attributes the same recipe to the 2.4T flagship. The 27B dense we serve is unconfirmed, and Alibaba has confirmed no technical report is coming for it.

Why this matters to us: Muon's orthogonalized updates should flatten singular spectra — and flatter spectra mean lower low-rank capture, i.e. our correction method works measurably worse on Muon-trained checkpoints. That was a curiosity when the only Muon model was Moonshot's Moonlight; it is a first-order question when it may describe the default model on our own rig. The literature has not touched "does Muon provenance change post-training quantizability" — four Muon papers appeared in the window (2606.04058, 2606.00371, 2509.23106, 2605.19282) and none do PTQ. E30 ran the same day this report was written — a paired stable-rank census of Qwen3.8-27B vs Qwen3.6-27B across 256 matched 2-D tensors — and came back null on both pre-registered metrics: no flattening (+0.0015 ± 0.0035). Either the 27B dense was not Muon-trained, or the effect washes out at convergence. Operationally that is good news (no capture penalty on our default lineup); scientifically the "does Muon change quantizability" question stays open and unclaimed, with Moonlight as the clean test bench and our null as its published-ready control.

4.2 · A correctness bug in our architecture's forward pass

llama.cpp merged a fix (#28068, build b10829) for a Gated-DeltaNet normalization that diverged from Qwen's reference kernels — mean-KLD 0.00175 on Qwen3.8-27B against the unfixed graph. Every build we have ever run computes the deviant norm for the qwen35 family, i.e. both our champion and our default. Paired same-build comparisons survive (both arms share the graph); absolute claims against reference implementations do not. Standing rule adopted: never mix pre- and post-b10829 rows in one table, and the kernel branch rebases before the next measurement campaign.

4.3 · The NVFP4 single-stream bet is still a bet

The merged NVFP4 performance PR (#25730) turned out to be prompt-processing only (1.03–1.07×); decode — the number that matters for single-user serving — is untouched, and the PRs that would move it (#26311 weight repack, #25153 imatrix-aware scale search) remain open. Independent confirmation of our own measurement that K-quants beat NVFP4 at batch 1. Meanwhile an industry guide states flatly that NVFP4-vs-Q4_K_M quality "is unsettled — no independent benchmark has run it." We have that benchmark instrumented.

4.4 · Vendors converge on our type-allocation finding

Three independent production systems now put their lowest bits on experts only and leave everything else high-precision — Kimi K3 (MXFP4-QAT), GLM-5, and NVIDIA's DeepSeek-V4-NVFP4 checkpoints. And NVIDIA's production recipe carries speculative/MTP heads through unquantized — first-party validation of the calibration-blind-spot argument we've been assembling for llama.cpp upstream. New blind-spot species keep appearing: Qwen's Gated-Residual gates and host-side n-gram tables, Tencent Hunyuan's separate 3.8B MTP layer shipped in vendor GGUFs.

4.5 · The vendor baseline to beat changed

ModelOpt v0.47 productized Four-Over-Six block quantization (per-block dynamic-range selection) plus a 34×-faster fused Hessian scale search. Our NVFP4 codec lane now scores against that, not against naive RTN — alongside OCGQuant's outlier-companion grouping (2609.00066) and SOAR's scale optimization (2605.12245), all replayable offline in our whitened metric from cached statistics.

4.6 · A rival correction geometry appeared

AWSRC (2608.23144) repairs frozen INT4 backbones with seed-generated bases — sign patterns, permutations, Hadamard columns — storing near-zero basis bytes, only coefficients. It claims to beat low-rank at equal bytes (at 3B, unwhitened, PPL-metric). This is both the closest published attack on our equivalence-class claim and a gift: seeded bases are a candidate fourth member of the class — or its first escapee — testable in hours from our cached 27B Grams. That duel is now queued.

4.7 · The GSQ head-to-head got cheaper and sharper

GSQ — the other group refining shipped GGUFs — is frozen at v2 with zero follow-up citations picking up the thread. Its training loop requires H100-class hardware even for 8B models, so our mandatory head-to-head (T1.17) runs against their released checkpoints, and the asymmetry becomes a measured positioning fact: their method cannot run on the consumer cards it produces artifacts for; ours runs where it serves. Their lab's earlier produce-time GGUF toolkit joins the comparison as a baseline row.

4.8 · The artifacts came to us

Unsloth now exports NVFP4/FP8/imatrix GGUFs directly, and community NVFP4-GGUF builds of both Qwen3.6-27B and Qwen3.8-27B are on Hugging Face. The frozen-FP4-base experiments (E16) no longer require us to produce the base artifact — we can download the exact thing users would serve and correct it.
