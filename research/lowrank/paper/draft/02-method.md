# 2. Method

The pipeline [F1] has four stages: capture second-moment statistics on calibration text (§2.1); fit whitened corrections in closed form (§2.2) and/or re-round the base's codes on their frozen grids (§2.3); optionally alternate quantizer and corrector (§2.4); serve through llama.cpp's existing adapter path or our fused kernels (§5). All quality claims follow the measurement protocol of §2.5.

## 2.1 Statistics capture: activation Grams, MoE extension, gradient Grams

**Input-side Grams.** llama-imatrix already accumulates diag(E[x²]) per weight column — the diagonal of the input second-moment matrix, and exactly the diagonal scaling LQER (arXiv:2402.02446) and QERA-approx (arXiv:2410.06040) employ. We extended it (a local, env-gated C++ patch; ~140 lines) to accumulate the full Gram G = Σ x xᵀ per *distinct layer input*: attn_k's input covers q/k/v, ffn_up's covers gate/up, attn_output and ffn_down get their own — four Grams per layer. Accumulation is fp32 with a hand-rolled threaded rank-k update (no BLAS dependency, honoring llama.cpp's no-third-party rule), written beside the normal imatrix. Validation: diag(G)/count matches the stock imatrix to median relative error 2e-8; exact symmetry; positive semidefinite [source: research/lowrank/experiments/10-full-covariance/README.md]. Inputs wider than 8192 dimensions are captured block-diagonally (8 blocks) — a memory-driven approximation whose consequences §2.2 makes explicit.

**MoE extension.** The MUL_MAT_ID (expert-routed) branch required its own accumulator: expert-slot-mixed rows must be unpacked per (token, slot) pair before the rank-k update, and gate/up stacks share a bitwise-identical routed input (verified), so one pooled Gram per stack input suffices. We capture *pooled* Grams — the union of routed inputs — rather than per-expert Grams, for a statistical reason as much as a storage one: at 16 calibration chunks, 65,536 routed pairs across 256 experts leave ~256 samples per expert for a 2048-dimensional Gram (rank-deficient by 8x), while the pooled Gram is full-rank; and the shared-basis hypothesis §4.5 tests is precisely a statement about the pooled distribution. Validation gates (symmetry exact, PSD, pooled diagonal matches the per-expert imatrix sums to 1.3e-4, counts exact) all pass on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B [source: research/lowrank/experiments/23-moe/REPORT-capture.md].

**Output-gradient Grams.** For two-sided (Kronecker-Fisher) metrics we built llama-gradmatrix, which runs llama.cpp's own training loop forward+backward with learning disabled and captures T = Σ g gᵀ per tensor, where g is the loss gradient at the pre-activation output row, hooked at the OUT_PROD nodes of the backward graph. Two structural repairs were required: llama.cpp's trainer never backpropagates through the KV cache (ggml_set_rows has no backward, and the attention read is edge-disconnected from the write — independently found; upstream issue #21037 reports the same seam), which we bypass by feeding k/v directly to attention when the whole window is one micro-batch; and several ops on the hybrid delta-net architectures have no backward, handled by an explicit, disclosed gradient cut that stops flow through higher layers' token mixing while keeping the residual path intact. All captures pass PSD/symmetry/count gates; the 0.8B capture's gradient cut biases exactly the 18 linear-attention layers, a caveat that becomes load-bearing in §4.6 [source: research/lowrank/experiments/20-gradgram/REPORT.md].

## 2.2 Whitened low-rank correction: closed forms and the blocked-whitening caveat

Given residual R = W_ref − dequant(Q) and an invertible whitener (L = damped Cholesky factor of G/count; damping 0.01·mean-diag, the GPTQ/CLoQ convention, arXiv:2210.17323, arXiv:2501.18475; the Cholesky-of-Gram whitening route is numerically viable at LLM scale by SVD-LLM's theorem, arXiv:2403.07378 — citation added 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review C1), the rank-r corrector minimizes the activation-weighted error phi(Delta) = ‖(R − Delta) L‖_F². The general two-sided weighted problem is NP-hard (Gillis-Glineur, arXiv:1012.0197); the one-sided problem has the classical closed form: whiten Rd = R L, truncate, unwhiten. We use the *projection form*: B = U_r (top-r left singular vectors of Rd), A = Bᵀ R. This is exactly optimal — for any invertible right whitener the unwhitening factor cancels, verified numerically to 7e-15 against the explicit argmin [source: research/lowrank/review/adversarial-code.md §A3] — and has the practical virtue that A is computed against the raw residual, so no ill-conditioned solve appears. Factors are computed by randomized SVD (Halko-Martinsson-Tropp, arXiv:0909.4061; oversampling 32, 4 power iterations) with streaming per-tensor dequantization, making a 27B extraction a ~270-second job.

**The blocked-whitening caveat, stated where the label is used.** For inputs above 8192 dimensions (27B ffn_down: 25,600) the captured Gram is block-diagonal, and the projection form is exactly optimal *for the block-diagonal surrogate metric*, not for the true E[xxᵀ]. A synthetic cross-correlated repro measured a 25% excess of true-metric loss for the blocked optimum versus the full argmin [source: research/lowrank/review/adversarial-code.md §B1]. Every "full covariance" 27B row in this paper therefore means: full covariance at ≤8192-dim inputs, 8-block-diagonal for ffn_down. End-to-end measurements are unaffected (they score the artifact), but the mechanism claim "the metric was the bottleneck" is untested against a genuinely full metric at that width.

**Two-sided variant.** With both S and T captured, B = L_T^{-ᵀ} U_r and A solved per block against L_S — matching the closed-form argmin to 1.3e-15 — with a factor-balancing step (equalize ‖B_i‖, ‖A_i‖; product provably unchanged, drift 9e-16) that exists because unbalanced un-whitening once overflowed F16 to 9,548 infs and a NaN serve [source: research/lowrank/review/adversarial-code.md §A4; JOURNAL.md block 5].

**Factor density.** Factors ship as Q8_0 (per-32 fp16 scale + int8), which halves factor bytes at measured quality parity (r64-Q8 31.26 vs F16's 31.29 at 0.6B; r128-Q8 ≡ r128-F16 at 53% of the bytes) — the Delta-CoMe/ZeroQuant-V2 observation (arXiv:2406.08903, arXiv:2303.08302) confirmed in this pipeline; capture-per-byte doubles for free [source: research/lowrank/JOURNAL.md, hour 2-3]. Mixed per-tensor ranks fold the runtime's alpha/rank scaling into A exactly as llama.cpp's adapter loader expects (verified against llama-adapter.h semantics).

**Serving.** The corrected model is {quantized base GGUF} + {LoRA-form adapter GGUF}: y = Q·x + s·B·(A·x) is llama.cpp's stock build_lora_mm path, so the artifact serves on unmodified llama-server — at the stock adapter tax §5 prices and then removes. Capture, however, is *not* stock: every full-covariance number in this paper depends on our imatrix Gram patch, and gradient Grams on llama-gradmatrix. "Zero code changes" was true only of the diagonal v0.

## 2.3 Re-rounding as a closest-vector problem on frozen grids

A K-quant tensor is a lattice-like product code: per 16-element sub-block, a scale/min pair (the *grid*) plus low-bit *codes*. llama-quantize chooses both, rounding each element independently. We freeze the grid bytes exactly as shipped and re-choose only the codes to minimize the whitened objective — a constrained closest-vector problem on the frozen product grid. Our solver is the GPTQ error-feedback sweep, which is precisely Babai's nearest-plane algorithm under the Cholesky factorization of the inverse Gram: process columns in order, round the current column on its frozen grid, and propagate the scaled error to unprocessed columns via the upper-triangular factor U (inv(H) = UᵀU). Frozen grids place us in the setting where Bid-Up (arXiv:2606.01412) proves per-sweep non-increase of the objective.

Implementation facts that carried published numbers: the Q2_K codec was mirrored bit-exactly from gguf-py (byte-identical repack round-trip on real tensors); the triangular convention is load-bearing (a lower factor silently no-ops the feedback and reproduces llama-quantize's codes bit-for-bit — diagnosed when PPL matched to four decimals); PD failures escalate damping rather than falling back to a dense factorization that would break sweep causality; lazy-batch blocking gives 8x speed at bit-identical output [source: research/lowrank/experiments/13-rerounder/README.md]. The Kronecker (two-sided) generalization — a nested row-within-column sweep under U_S ⊗ U_T, validated code-bit-exact against brute-force GPTQ on the explicit Kronecker factor — is derived in E24 together with a small negative theorem worth keeping: a *diagonal* output-side weighting is provably a no-op for frozen-grid per-element rounding (each row's argmin is scale-invariant), so off-diagonal feedback is the only channel through which an output metric can move any code [source: research/lowrank/experiments/24-yaqa-lite/README.md].

The output is byte-compatible with the input format: same size, same type tags, servable by every existing binary. This is the property that makes §3's result a *deployment* result and not just a benchmark.

## 2.4 Alternation and its convergence certificate

Correction quality depends on the base; the base's grid choices depend on what the corrector will absorb. The alternation Q_{t+1} = quant(W − C_t), C_{t+1} = corr(W − Q_{t+1}) — LoftQ's template (arXiv:2310.08659) with llama-quantize as a *black-box* quantizer and the whitened closed form as the exact corrector step — is run with best-iterate tracking and a stop-on-worse rule. The theory note [source: research/lowrank/paper/theory-alternation-convergence.md] upgrades that rule to a certificate: (Claim 1) the quantizer's reachable output set is finite, the corrector step is an exact argmin, so the best-iterate value is non-increasing over a finite set and becomes constant after finitely many rounds — termination in the exact sense that no further round can improve the tracked iterate; (Claim 2) round 1 improves iff pre-subtracting the corrector steers the quantizer to a grid point whose residual is more rank-r-compressible in the whitened metric — measured true at both scales (whitened capture 0.37 → 0.46 → 0.51 across rounds at 0.6B). Claim 3, informal, explains the geometric decay of per-round gains via the composed map approaching a fixed point where no code crosses a decision boundary of the quantizer's internal metric psi — with one measured amendment: when the alternation was pushed to an 8-iteration cap at 0.8B (E26), *no* codes fixed point was reached within budget; 92/96 tensors entered a ~0.1% limit cycle at the floor, so what actually happens is best-iterate convergence onto a limit cycle, not a fixed point [source: research/lowrank/experiments/26-lloyd-gauge/README.md; measured behavior cited 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review S4c]. The certificate's best-iterate framing survives unchanged. The mismatch psi ≠ phi bounds achievable quality and motivates ProjQ-style metric surgery (arXiv:2606.00494), with the Bregman-damped scheme of arXiv:2507.09428 as the proven-descent variant. In practice T ≤ 2-3 rounds; deltas after round 2 are sub-noise.

## 2.5 Measurement protocol (methodology v2)

Every result in §3-§5 either follows these standards or is labeled legacy [source: research/lowrank/PROTOCOL.md]. (1) *Reference and provenance*: the reference is the specifically *targeted* artifact — BF16 wherever it exists, and where it does not (the MoE of §4.5, for which no BF16 exists on our hardware) the strongest vendor artifact, stated per-table; every base and adapter derives from the reference in one quantization step; mixed-provenance rows never share a table without a provenance column. *(Targeted-reference rule stated here 2026-08-04 — §4.5's vendor-Q4_K_M scoring otherwise read as a violation of this section; round-2 paper review R4.)* (2) *Metrics*: mean KL divergence and top-1 agreement versus reference logits (≥40 chunks; the 27B and MoE tables run 20 chunks under a disclosed waiver — those rows settle *orderings* via paired statistics, and the 100-chunk extension is a queued repair, ABLATION-PLAN T1.5/T1.16 — disclosure added 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review R4) with printed standard errors — KLD because it tracks answer flips at Spearman 0.981 (arXiv:2407.09141) and is the llama.cpp maintainers' preferred currency (discussion #4110) — with full-corpus perplexity secondary and a stated silent-zone caveat near baseline (arXiv:2606.19558). (3) *Paired statistics*: any comparison inside 2 sigma unpaired is decided by the paired per-chunk difference (mean ± sem of Δ), recovered from llama-perplexity's per-chunk streams; rival configs share eval tokens and reference logits, so pairing has materially more power than the unpaired sigmas our first-night claims leaned on. (4) *Comparisons*: byte-fair means total bytes (base+adapter) *and* stated VRAM; the ladder gets its full lineup — K-quants, I-quants, and the mixed-quant *interpolation control* at the matched byte point — at every scale. (5) *Speed*: same-session paired A/B only; boost drift documented (up to 9% between sessions on identical configs); stock versus fused-kernel numbers labeled; the serving tax always priced beside byte-fair quality claims. (6) *Claims*: "law" requires ≥3 scale points with a stated functional form; otherwise "observation." Novelty claims require a tracker sweep, not only arXiv — two of our campaign's "discoveries" (the MTP-imatrix blind spot; the KV-backward seam) were already reported upstream (#23476/#23575; #21037), and are handled here as independent confirmations.
