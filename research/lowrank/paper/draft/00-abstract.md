# The Free Lever and the Measured Walls: Re-Rounding, Whitened Low-Rank Correction, and the Limits of Post-Training Quantization Repair in llama.cpp

## Abstract

Consumer GPU economics are compute-rich and memory-poor: cards such as the RTX 5090 pair formidable processors with VRAM pools too small for frontier models. We measure, on production llama.cpp artifacts, what post-training repair of aggressive quantization can and cannot buy under that constraint. Our first finding is a deployment premise inversion: batch-1 decode is memory-bandwidth-bound, so a 27B model quantized from 22.4 GB to 10.9 GB decodes *faster* (61 to 99.7 tok/s), making accuracy — not compute — the only price of compression [source: research/lowrank/RESULTS_ROLLUP.md]. We then contribute one lever that is free *at fixed calibration breadth* and an audit of the levers that are not. The free lever is frozen-grid re-rounding: keeping llama-quantize's per-block grids byte-for-byte and re-choosing only the codes under a captured activation-covariance metric — a closest-vector sweep in the Babai/GPTQ (arXiv:2210.17323) lineage — improves a Q2_K model by 15% perplexity and 34% KL divergence at *identical bytes*, resolved at 30-45 paired sigma, emitting a byte-compatible GGUF that runs unmodified on every deployed llama.cpp stack [source: research/lowrank/experiments/24-yaqa-lite/README.md]. The lever's own caveat is measured, not hypothetical: calibrated narrowly, it *anti-generalizes* — the wikitext-calibrated re-round is worse than the untouched model on a held-out code corpus (PPL 3.470 vs 3.083) — and a mixed-calibration variant recovers both corpora at a modest on-distribution cost, so the claim is "improves what it is calibrated on," conditioned on calibration breadth and a held-out gate [source: research/lowrank/paper/DEPLOYABLE-WINS.md]. The walls: weight matrices and their quantization residuals are energy-full-rank; whitened low-rank correction is real at every scale but its fixed-rank capture collapses with width (a two-point observation, not a law); and against llama.cpp's own quant ladder the corrected configurations never rise above the ladder's mixed-quant interpolation line at equal bytes. At 27B, re-derived provenance-pure from BF16 with paired per-chunk statistics, the corrected flagship reaches paired-tie parity with the adjacent rung (the legacy 1σ "victory" flipped sign under clean provenance), and a pure-quantization interpolation control we build at the same byte point matches-or-beats it — adapter-free and serving-tax-free [source: research/lowrank/experiments/27-bf16-rederivation/README.md]. At 0.6-0.8B the corrections win only from below, at a 20-40% stock-serving decode tax. At MoE scale, expert residuals defeat shared-basis amortization (shared-basis capture barely above the random-basis floor), and per-expert correction loses to a trivial quant-promotion control at 6.9 paired sigma [source: research/lowrank/experiments/23-moe/README.md]. All quality results are measured on the calibration distribution (wikitext-2 train/test split); held-out and task evaluation are the stated gating repairs (§6). We supply mechanisms for each wall, a convergence certificate for quantizer-corrector alternation, a fused-kernel trilogy that cuts the adapter tax (+79% decode at 0.6B) plus an allocator fix worth +19.6% to any CUDA llama.cpp user, and a measurement protocol (paired per-chunk statistics, interpolation controls, provenance-pure references) distilled from an adversarial review of our own first claims.
