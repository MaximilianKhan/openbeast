# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 The compute-rich, memory-poor regime

Current GPU economics gave consumers a strange machine: processors of datacenter caliber strapped to VRAM pools sized for last decade's models. An RTX 5090 offers Blackwell tensor throughput behind 32 GB of memory; the models people want to serve on it are tens to hundreds of gigabytes at training precision. The classical framing of model compression for such hardware — trade compute time for memory by reconstructing compressed weights on the fly — turns out to be empirically backwards at the batch sizes consumer serving actually runs. Single-stream decode is memory-bandwidth-bound: every token reads the full weight set, and arithmetic units idle while HBM streams. We measured the consequence directly on a production 27B model (Qwen3.6-27B; the served checkpoint is "heretic-v2", a community finetune of the base model with MTP layers preserved — a model-provenance note: all quantization/correction machinery here is checkpoint-agnostic, and the camera-ready ladder can be re-derived on the base checkpoint *— footnote added 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review R8*): the Q6_K reference occupies 23.6 GB of VRAM and decodes at 61 tok/s; the same model at Q2_K occupies 13.0 GB and decodes at 99.7 tok/s [source: research/lowrank/RESULTS_ROLLUP.md]. Smaller weights are not merely feasible — they are *faster*. The trade was never compute-for-memory; it is accuracy-for-memory, with compute as the free resource available to buy accuracy back.

That inversion defines this paper's question: given that compute is free and bytes are everything, *how much quality can post-training machinery recover per byte — or per zero bytes — on top of a mature quantization stack?* We answer it on llama.cpp, the dominant consumer inference engine, whose K-quant and I-quant ladder (imatrix-weighted per-block quantizers spanning roughly 1.5-6.5 bits per weight) is the strongest deployed baseline in this regime and, as we will show repeatedly, a far harder rival than the literature's round-to-nearest strawmen.

## 1.2 What this paper is not

This is not a paper claiming low-rank factorization compresses LLMs. It does not, and we can show exactly why and where. A full singular-value census of every tensor in our smallest subject finds that capturing 95% of Frobenius energy requires 50-80% of full rank on every projection type — compression at that rank saves approximately nothing, and for V/FFN projections it costs memory [source: research/lowrank/experiments/01-svd-spectrum/README.md]. The quantization *residual* R = W − dequant(Q), the object the quantization-error-correction lineage (ZeroQuant-V2/LoRC, arXiv:2303.08302; LQER, arXiv:2402.02446; QERA, arXiv:2410.06040; EoRA, arXiv:2410.21271; CALDERA, arXiv:2405.18886) corrects, is even less structured: near-isotropic, with 90% of its energy needing ~62% of full rank [source: research/lowrank/experiments/03-residual-rank/README.md]. Both nulls confirm the Marchenko-Pastur picture LQER published, and RILQ's published diagnosis of the same wall — 2-bit quantization error is inherently high-rank, and layer-local low-rank correction saturates (arXiv:2412.01129): unweighted SVD capture of a quantization residual is approximately r/d, i.e. nothing at deployable ranks.

Neither is this a paper claiming a new quantization method beats llama.cpp's ladder. Our strongest corrected configuration at 27B, re-derived provenance-pure from BF16 with paired per-chunk statistics, reaches paired-tie parity with the adjacent rung Q3_K_S (KLD t = 0.6, top-1 t = 1.0, a real PPL edge at t = −2.5); the legacy unpaired 1σ "margin" flipped sign under clean provenance; a pure-quant interpolation control built at the same byte point matches-or-beats the corrected config; and the I-quant rung IQ3_XS beats both at fewer bytes, adapter-free (paired t = −2.8 vs Q3_K_S) [source: research/lowrank/experiments/27-bf16-rederivation/README.md]. *(Sentence updated 2026-08-04: it previously quoted the retracted E10-sourced legacy numbers — "1.0 sigma unpaired, top-1 exactly tied at 88.039%" — which E27 superseded and whose top-1 coincidence does not exist in clean-provenance data; round-2 paper review M3/S1.)* We state this plainly because our own first drafts did not: an adversarial review of the campaign's claims [source: research/lowrank/review/adversarial-claims.md, review/adversarial-stats.md] killed "first ladder victory" framings, two-point "laws," and unqualified compression multiples, and the corrected framings — parity, wins-from-below, priced serving taxes — govern every sentence here.

## 1.3 What this paper contributes

**(1) A zero-byte quality lever, conditioned on calibration breadth.** llama-quantize picks good per-block grids but rounds every element independently. Freezing those grids byte-for-byte and re-choosing only the codes under a captured full activation-covariance metric — a closest-vector-problem sweep in the GPTQ/Babai lineage (arXiv:2210.17323), applied for the first time to llama.cpp's deployed K-quant format — improves Qwen3.5-0.8B at Q2_K by −15% perplexity, −34% KL divergence, and +6.3 points top-1 agreement at *identical bytes*, resolved at t = 30-45 in paired per-chunk statistics against a BF16 truth reference [source: research/lowrank/experiments/24-yaqa-lite/README.md]. The output is a byte-compatible standard GGUF: it runs unmodified on every llama.cpp build and every downstream application, with zero adoption friction. Two honest bounds. First, it recovers roughly half of the quality gap to the next quant tier, not the tier itself; gains concentrate at 2-3-bit tiers and shrink toward Q4. Second — measured, and load-bearing for any deployment claim: the gain is *given sufficiently broad calibration*. Narrow-calibration re-rounding anti-generalizes: the wikitext-calibrated artifact is worse than bare on a held-out code corpus (PPL 3.470 vs 3.083), and a mixed 2:1 calibration rescues both corpora at a modest on-distribution cost (§3.1) [source: research/lowrank/paper/DEPLOYABLE-WINS.md]. Millions of deployed K-quant files carry this headroom today; we are not aware of a tool in this class (our tracker sweeps have missed tracker-resident prior art before, and the sweep record is disclosed as incomplete). *(Clause conditioned + novelty softened 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review R2/R3a; "~40%" was ~50% on the recorded numbers per round-2 experiments review F13.)*

**(2) A paired-statistics audit of every within-paradigm correction lever, at three dense scales and one MoE.** Metric quality (diagonal versus full-covariance whitening), quantizer-corrector co-design (alternation, with a termination certificate), factor density (Q8_0 factors: capture-per-byte doubled free), rank allocation (measured sensitivity helps; energy water-filling is actively harmful), structural variants (column patches, shared bases, task conditioning), and base-format composition (K-quants, I-quants, IQ1_S, NVFP4). Each lever is real; each shrinks with scale; and the audit's negative results — the shared-basis null, the MoE control defeat, the anti-helpfulness of a misestimated Kronecker metric, the Lloyd fixed-point null and the equalization anti-result of E26 (§3.4, §4.6) — are, ironically, its most statistically secure findings [source: research/lowrank/review/adversarial-stats.md]. *(E26 nulls added to the list 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review S4.)*

**(3) Mechanisms for the failures.** Fixed-rank whitened capture fell approximately fivefold when width grew fivefold across our two diagonal-whitened scale points — consistent with a constant-cardinality outlier head (LLM.int8(), arXiv:2208.07339; Massive Activations, arXiv:2402.17762) diluted by growing bulk dimension, and with the rank-proportional-to-width bounds of CALDERA (arXiv:2405.18886) and the intrinsic-LoRA lower bound (arXiv:2606.01412). We are explicit that this is a two-point *observation*, not a law: the constant varies 9.6-20.8 across whiteners and bases in our own data [source: research/lowrank/review/adversarial-stats.md §F6; RESULTS_ROLLUP.md correction 5 gives the equivalent 0.36-0.60×d/r parameterization — citation fixed 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review M4]. The design rule the campaign distilled — *correct where geometry favors correction (rank a large fraction of width); spend base bits where it does not* — is validated constructively by a geometry-aware mixed base at 27B and destructively by the IQ1_S asymptote experiment, where even 2.5 GB of full-covariance factors cannot substitute for carrier bits [source: research/lowrank/experiments/15-iq1-carrier/README.md].

**(4) The systems layer that makes correction servable.** llama.cpp's stock LoRA path prices any adapter at a 20-40% decode tax that the bare quant rungs never pay. A three-phase fused-kernel campaign eliminates most of it (+44%, +62%, +79% adapter decode at 0.6B; +2.6% point-estimate at 27B against a measured +4.9% ceiling), yielding two transferable CUDA idioms — prefetch static operands before the grid dependency sync, and keep epilogues register-neutral by parking state in shared memory — and, en route, an allocator-level concurrency fix worth +19.6% decode to any CUDA llama.cpp user with graph optimization, adapter or not [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT-PHASE3.md, experiments/25-alloc-concurrency/REPORT.md].

**(5) A measurement protocol earned the hard way.** Calibration-distribution evaluation, missing interpolation controls, unpaired sub-2-sigma margins, and GPU boost drift each produced a headline our review subsequently retracted. Methodology v2 — BF16-pure single-step provenance, paired per-chunk statistics for every margin inside 2 sigma unpaired, ladder comparisons that include the mixed-quant interpolation control, and serving tax priced beside every byte-fair quality claim — is stated in §2 and applied to all new results [source: research/lowrank/PROTOCOL.md].

## 1.4 Relation to prior work, in one paragraph

Our pipeline is deliberately built from published mathematics: the whitened closed forms are QERA's (arXiv:2410.06040) Theorem 1/2 pair, whose diagonal special case is literally llama.cpp's imatrix statistic; the alternation protocol descends from LoftQ (arXiv:2310.08659) with CALDERA's best-iterate practice; the re-rounder is GPTQ's sweep on a frozen grid, the setting where Bid-Up (arXiv:2606.01412) proves monotonicity. What is new is where it is measured and what that measurement shows: same-pipeline full-versus-diagonal and correction-versus-ladder ablations on llama.cpp's production K/I-quants at 0.6B, 0.8B, 27B, and a 35B MoE, against the strongest deployed baseline rather than round-to-nearest — including, uniquely in this lineage, *mixed-quant interpolation controls* and I-quant rivals at matched bytes, comparisons none of CALDERA/LQER/EoRA report against their RTN/GPTQ baselines (and which, at 27B and MoE scale, our own corrections lose); the frozen-grid re-rounder as a deployable tool class; the MoE expert-orthogonality negative; the fused-GEMV serving path the field's own artifacts conspicuously lack (EoRA ships unfused in HF/vLLM; MiLo, arXiv:2504.02658, states its compensator is unfused; CALDERA's Table 6 records the decode loss). *(Interpolation-control differentiator foregrounded 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review R3b.)* The upstream conversation this feeds — llama.cpp discussion #8831, where an LQER-style correction was proposed in 2024 and predicted not to help mature quants (ik_llama.cpp #15) — receives here its first systematic empirical answer: the skeptic was mostly right about correction versus the ladder, and wrong about the grid itself, whose codes carry a free 15-34%.
