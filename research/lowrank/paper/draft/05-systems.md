# 5. The systems layer: pricing and removing the serving tax

## 5.1 The tax, stated first

Every quality result in §4 that involves an adapter carries a cost the bare quant rungs do not: on stock llama.cpp, the LoRA path adds two unfused GEMV launches per projection, and batch-1 decode pays for them dearly — −33% to −39% at 0.6B (667 → 444/407 tok/s; 407/667 = −39% — the "−41%" previously printed was an arithmetic slip inherited from the round-1 claims review, fixed 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review M6) and ~−20% at 27B, while the published record shows the same pathology industry-wide. One reconciliation note so the paper carries a single tax story: §5.2's "−45% → −20%" states the tax of the *Phase-2 session* against its own higher bare baseline (boost/context drift between sessions, documented in §5.1); the −33/−39% figures are the E04-session pairs — same pathology, different session baselines *(footnote added per M6)* (adapter ops rank-independent and launch-bound at batch 1: Punica, arXiv:2310.18547; AdaFuse, arXiv:2603.11873; FlashSVD v1.5's 1,174 launches/token, arXiv:2605.08314; CALDERA's own Table 6 decode loss, arXiv:2405.18886). Per methodology v2, this tax is priced beside every byte-fair claim: "competes with the rung on stock llama.cpp" is not currently a true sentence. The kernels below remove most of the tax — on our local, unmerged patches — and are digit-identical in PPL and kill-switched at every stage. All speed verdicts are same-session A/B pairs; between-session GPU boost drift reached 9% on identical configs and struck one previously-published headline cell (86.3 tok/s → 82.3 real) [source: research/lowrank/review/adversarial-stats.md §F2-F3].

## 5.2 The fused-kernel trilogy

**Phase 1 — window fusion.** The 5-node subgraph llama.cpp emits per adapted projection ({W·x, A·x, B·t, scale, add}) is pattern-matched in the CUDA backend and collapsed to two kernels: B·t + scale + add ride the quantized-GEMV (mmvq) epilogue. Result: digit-identical PPL, +10% at r16, exact parity at r64 — and a falsified premise. Removing 588 of 784 extra kernels/token moved r64 by ~0: under Blackwell CUDA-graph replay, tiny launches were nearly free. Under GPU contention, however, the same fusion was worth +42% (single uncontrolled run; direction real, magnitude unreproduced) [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT.md].

**Phase 2 — fold A into the activation quantize; find the real wall.** The design template is SVDQuant's (arXiv:2411.05007): fold Aᵀx into the activation-quantize stage and B·t into the GEMM epilogue, the fusion that took their low-rank branch from +50% latency to 5-10% — re-derived here for the GEMV/batch-1 regime their diffusion-serving engine does not cover *(citation added 2026-08-04 per round-2 paper review C1)*. Folding t = A·x into the pre-existing quantize_q8_1 launch (dedicated trailing thread blocks; no atomics, no new graph nodes) plus a redesigned epilogue took r64 decode from 476 to 685 tok/s (+44%), collapsing the adapter tax from −45% to −20%, rank-flat and bit-identical. The diagnosis that paid: the constant cost was never launch overhead but *tail-load serialization* — the epilogue loaded B and t after the main dot loop, adding a full memory-latency round to every mmvq's critical path (and four serial rounds on the small-K variant). The generalizable idiom: **prefetch static operands before the PDL grid-dependency sync** — weights can legally stream into registers while cudaGridDependencySynchronize waits for the producer grid, converting exposed latency into overlap. A long-standing rank-64 speed anomaly dissolved with the same fix [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT-PHASE2.md].

**Phase 2B — quantized factors; fusion is not free once the rival is dp4a.** Accepting Q8_0 factors in both fused paths gave +62% at 0.6B (394 → 638) but initially *regressed* 27B by 6.6%: unfused Q8 B·t already rides an int8 dp4a kernel upstream, while the fused epilogue's per-row float tax grows with model size and launch savings do not. Two empirical size gates (rows x rank; fold-K) restored a no-regression +0.4% at 27B — an honest crossover measurement rather than a win [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT-PHASE2B.md].

**Phase 3 — quantized-t sidecar and the occupancy law.** The planned fix (a q8 sidecar of t enabling an int8 dp4a epilogue) was built and is digit-clean — and turned out to be the small half. The real 27B tax was *register pressure*: the fused instantiation carried ~19 extra registers (61 → 80), dropping resident blocks from 4 to 3 for every fused W kernel across ~2.8M output rows/token. Splitting the LoRA template off the GLU fusion machinery and moving the entire adapter dot before the main loop — its result parked in shared memory across the existing barrier, no adapter register live in the main loop — restored lean occupancy (62 registers). Final same-session pairs: 0.6B 391 → 702 tok/s (+79%); 27B 84.7 → 86.9 (+2.6% point estimate; against realistic 1-2% run noise this single pair is ~1-1.5 sigma, direction corroborated by a rank-0 ceiling probe at 89.4 and consistent fused>unfused spreads; the measured ceiling for any epilogue is +4.9%). The second transferable idiom: **epilogues of memory-bound GEMV kernels must be register-neutral in the main loop; shared memory is the only safe place to carry epilogue state.** A corollary with deployment value: launch savings scale inversely with clocks, so fused margins grow under contention and thermal limits — the regime real multi-tenant serving lives in [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT-PHASE3.md].

## 5.3 The allocator fix: +19.6% for everyone

llama.cpp's GGML_CUDA_GRAPH_OPT stream concurrency was being correctly *rejected* on adapter rigs: ggml-alloc keeps one global free-list, so a buffer freed inside one fork branch is handed to a tensor in a concurrent branch — a genuine cross-stream write-after-read hazard (an earlier interleave attempt corrupted generation silently). The fix is allocator-level branch-window pinning: a tensor flag marks fork-join regions; frees inside a region are deferred to the join; an in-place guard prevents the last consumer of a fork tensor from stealing a buffer other streams still read. Cost: zero measured buffer growth. Verification: the full backend test suite (12,996 cases) passes; PPL digit-identical and 200-token greedy generation *string-identical* on/off across all three rigs — the check the earlier corruption escaped. Result: 0.6B base decode 794 → 950 tok/s (+19.6%, reproducing the Phase-2 prize with events validated rather than lucky); +7-12% with the adapter under residual host load; 27B within noise (streams cover only the hybrid's 1-in-4 full-attention layers, and 5120-10240-row GEMVs already fill the GPU — concurrency pays on launch-bound small kernels). A bonus root-cause: the 27B rig had been forming zero stream regions because pair-seeding rejected Q8_0 A factors — absence of concurrency logs is a distinct failure mode from invalid events [source: research/lowrank/experiments/25-alloc-concurrency/REPORT.md]. This fix is unrelated to compression and benefits any CUDA llama.cpp user with graph optimization; with the GRAPH_OPT interleave-restore bug found and fixed in Phase 2 (a silent 552-tok/s regression mode), it forms the upstream-shaped half of the patch set [source: research/lowrank/paper/DEPLOYABLE-WINS.md].

## 5.4 Serving-tax accounting after the trilogy

Where the ledger stands [F11 — the serving-tax ledger figure; renumbered 2026-08-04: F5/F6 are the tok/s-vs-rank plot and the fused-kernel schematic in the figure plan, not this table; round-2 paper review §1.3]: at 0.6B the adapter tax on our patched build is ~20% (down from 45%), and the free +19.6% base-model lever stacks on top; at 27B the flagship's fused decode (86.9 point-estimate) sits within a bounded +4.9% ceiling of unfused, i.e. the tax at production scale is already small once factors are Q8 — the remaining gap to bare-rung serving is the adapter's extra VRAM (~0.7 GB class) and the residual per-token latency chain, which the megakernel literature prices at 1.3-2 µs/node (Mirage MPK, arXiv:2512.22219; Hazy megakernels) and which allocator-level concurrency, not further fusion, is the identified next lever. Every number in this section is reproducible from the per-phase reports with pinned build SHA (llama.cpp 0ef6e55ed), kill switches, and same-session pairing; the quiet-host 27B speed verdict is the one open measurement gate [source: research/lowrank/experiments/25-alloc-concurrency/REPORT.md].
