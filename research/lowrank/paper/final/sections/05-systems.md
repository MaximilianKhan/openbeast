# 5. The systems layer: pricing and removing the serving tax

## 5.1 The tax, stated first

Every quality result in §4 that involves an adapter carries a cost the bare quant rungs do not. On stock llama.cpp the LoRA path adds two unfused GEMV launches per projection, and batch-1 decode pays for them: −33% to −39% at 0.6B (667 → 444 and 407 tok/s) and about −20% at 27B. The published record shows the same pathology elsewhere: adapter operations are rank-independent and launch-bound at batch 1 (Punica, arXiv:2310.18547; AdaFuse, arXiv:2603.11873; FlashSVD v1.5's 1,174 launches per token, arXiv:2605.08314; CALDERA's Table 6, arXiv:2405.18886). Under the protocol of §2.5 this tax is priced beside every byte-fair claim: "competes with the rung on stock llama.cpp" is not currently a true sentence. The kernels below remove most of the tax on local, unmerged patches, are digit-identical in perplexity, and are kill-switched at every stage. All speed verdicts are same-session A/B pairs. Between-session GPU boost drift reached 9% on identical configurations and invalidated one headline cell from our first tables (86.3 tok/s, 82.3 on re-measurement); for the same reason the "−45% → −20%" of §5.2 is stated against that session's own bare baseline and differs from the 0.6B pairs quoted here [source: research/lowrank/review/adversarial-stats.md §F2-F3].

## 5.2 Three phases of kernel fusion

**Phase 1 — window fusion.** The 5-node subgraph llama.cpp emits per adapted projection ({W·x, A·x, B·t, scale, add}) is pattern-matched in the CUDA backend and collapsed to two kernels: B·t + scale + add ride the quantized-GEMV (mmvq) epilogue. Result: digit-identical PPL, +10% at r16, exact parity at r64 — and a falsified premise. Removing 588 of 784 extra kernels/token moved r64 by ~0: under Blackwell CUDA-graph replay, tiny launches were nearly free. Under GPU contention, however, the same fusion was worth +42% (single uncontrolled run; direction real, magnitude unreproduced) [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT.md].

**Phase 2 — fold A into the activation quantize; find the real wall.** The design template is SVDQuant's (arXiv:2411.05007): fold Aᵀx into the activation-quantize stage and B·t into the GEMM epilogue, the fusion that took their low-rank branch from +50% latency to 5-10% — re-derived here for the GEMV/batch-1 regime their diffusion-serving engine does not cover. Folding t = A·x into the pre-existing quantize_q8_1 launch (dedicated trailing thread blocks; no atomics, no new graph nodes) plus a redesigned epilogue took r64 decode from 476 to 685 tok/s (+44%), collapsing the adapter tax from −45% to −20%, rank-flat and bit-identical. The diagnosis that paid: the constant cost was never launch overhead but *tail-load serialization* — the epilogue loaded B and t after the main dot loop, adding a full memory-latency round to every mmvq's critical path (and four serial rounds on the small-K variant). The generalizable idiom: **prefetch static operands before the PDL grid-dependency sync** — weights can legally stream into registers while cudaGridDependencySynchronize waits for the producer grid, converting exposed latency into overlap. A long-standing rank-64 speed anomaly dissolved with the same fix [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT-PHASE2.md].

**Phase 2B — quantized factors; fusion is not free once the rival is dp4a.** Accepting Q8_0 factors in both fused paths gave +62% at 0.6B (394 → 638) but initially *regressed* 27B by 6.6%: unfused Q8 B·t already rides an int8 dp4a kernel upstream, while the fused epilogue's per-row float tax grows with model size and launch savings do not. Two empirical size gates (rows x rank; fold-K) restored a no-regression +0.4% at 27B — an honest crossover measurement rather than a win [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT-PHASE2B.md].

**Phase 3 — quantized-t sidecar and the occupancy law.** The planned fix (a q8 sidecar of t enabling an int8 dp4a epilogue) was built and is digit-clean — and turned out to be the small half. The real 27B tax was *register pressure*: the fused instantiation carried ~19 extra registers (61 → 80), dropping resident blocks from 4 to 3 for every fused W kernel across ~2.8M output rows/token. Splitting the LoRA template off the GLU fusion machinery and moving the entire adapter dot before the main loop — its result parked in shared memory across the existing barrier, no adapter register live in the main loop — restored lean occupancy (62 registers). Final same-session pairs: 0.6B 391 → 702 tok/s (+79%); 27B 84.7 → 86.9 (+2.6%, a single pair). Re-measured on a quiet host with interleaved blocks (N = 10, median and interquartile range), the 27B flagship decodes at 88.1 [88.0-88.3] fused against 85.8 [85.7-85.8] unfused: +2.7% with disjoint ranges, against a measured ceiling of +4.9% for any epilogue [source: research/lowrank/experiments/27-bf16-rederivation/r8-flagship-bench.txt]. The second transferable idiom: **epilogues of memory-bound GEMV kernels must be register-neutral in the main loop; shared memory is the only safe place to carry epilogue state.** A corollary with deployment value: launch savings scale inversely with clocks, so fused margins grow under contention and thermal limits — the regime real multi-tenant serving lives in [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT-PHASE3.md].

## 5.3 The allocator fix: +19.6% for everyone

llama.cpp's GGML_CUDA_GRAPH_OPT stream concurrency was being correctly *rejected* on adapter rigs: ggml-alloc keeps one global free-list, so a buffer freed inside one fork branch is handed to a tensor in a concurrent branch — a genuine cross-stream write-after-read hazard (an earlier interleave attempt corrupted generation silently). The fix is allocator-level branch-window pinning: a tensor flag marks fork-join regions; frees inside a region are deferred to the join; an in-place guard prevents the last consumer of a fork tensor from stealing a buffer other streams still read. Cost: zero measured buffer growth. Verification: the full backend test suite (12,996 cases) passes; PPL digit-identical and 200-token greedy generation *string-identical* on/off across all three rigs — the check the earlier corruption escaped. Result: 0.6B base decode 794 → 950 tok/s (+19.6%, reproducing the Phase-2 prize with events validated rather than lucky); +7-12% with the adapter under residual host load; 27B within noise (streams cover only the hybrid's 1-in-4 full-attention layers, and 5120-10240-row GEMVs already fill the GPU — concurrency pays on launch-bound small kernels). A bonus root-cause: the 27B rig had been forming zero stream regions because pair-seeding rejected Q8_0 A factors — absence of concurrency logs is a distinct failure mode from invalid events [source: research/lowrank/experiments/25-alloc-concurrency/REPORT.md]. This fix is unrelated to compression and benefits any CUDA llama.cpp user with graph optimization; with the GRAPH_OPT interleave-restore bug found and fixed in Phase 2 (a silent 552-tok/s regression mode), it forms the upstream-shaped half of the patch set [source: research/lowrank/paper/DEPLOYABLE-WINS.md].

## 5.4 Serving-tax accounting after fusion

Where the ledger stands: at 0.6B the adapter tax on our patched build is ~20% (down from 45%), and the free +19.6% base-model lever stacks on top; at 27B the flagship's fused decode (88.1 tok/s) sits within a bounded +4.9% ceiling of unfused, i.e. the tax at production scale is already small once factors are Q8 — the remaining gap to bare-rung serving is the adapter's extra VRAM (~0.7 GB class) and the residual per-token latency chain, which the megakernel literature prices at 1.3-2 µs/node (Mirage MPK, arXiv:2512.22219; Hazy megakernels) and which allocator-level concurrency, not further fusion, is the identified next lever. Every number in this section comes from the per-phase reports at a pinned build (llama.cpp 0ef6e55ed), with kill switches and same-session pairing. The patch set has since been rebased onto llama.cpp b10865 and is vendored with the experiment tree; no speed number here was re-measured after the rebase, and all of them are from one GPU [source: research/lowrank/experiments/25-alloc-concurrency/REPORT.md].
