## Abstract

We measure, on llama.cpp files of Qwen-family models [PENDING:G — and Llama-3.1-8B] on one GPU, what post-training repair of low-bit quantization buys, scoring every comparison against BF16 references on the calibration distribution and on two held-out corpora. (1) Frozen-grid re-rounding re-chooses only the codes on llama-quantize's grids. At identical bytes it lowers a Q2_K file's KL divergence (KLD) by a third at 0.8B [PENDING:B — and by X% at 27B]. The gain lands only on the kinds of text the calibration covers: calibrated on prose it makes code worse, and the reverse; a calibration mixing both improves all three corpora. A smaller stock IQ3_XXS file is still better at 0.8B. [PENDING:E — free-grid GPTQ result.] (2) Whitened low-rank correction improves its base on every corpus at 0.8B and 27B, by about half as much off the calibration distribution. Against the stock types it falls short: at 27B its 5% lead over the adjacent type holds only on the calibration distribution (reversed on one held-out corpus, unresolved on the other), and a same-byte file mixing stock types is better on every corpus, by a factor of three in KLD at 0.8B. (3) Against released GSQ-RCO checkpoints, KLD to the reference and likelihood of the text disagree within one run at a 2,048-token context: the trained files trade fidelity to BF16 for likelihood, so KLD ranks fidelity, not quality. [PENDING:F — capability replication.] (4) Fused adapter kernels raise 0.6B decode by 79% and 27B decode by 2.7% [source: research/lowrank/experiments/36-submission/PLAN.md; experiments/36-submission/RESULTS-A.md; experiments/36-submission/RESULTS-C.md; experiments/36-submission/RESULTS-D.md].
