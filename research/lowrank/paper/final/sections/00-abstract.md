## Abstract

We measure what post-training repair of low-bit llama.cpp quantization buys, on four Qwen-family models and Llama-3.1-8B, on one GPU, with every comparison scored against BF16 references on the calibration distribution and on two corpora no calibration touched. (1) Re-rounding: re-choosing a Q2_K file's codes under a captured activation-covariance metric (GPTQ's sweep on llama-quantize's frozen grid) lowers KL divergence (KLD) by a third at 0.8B, but at 8B and 27B it gives no resolved gain on the calibration distribution and harms both held-out corpora, although it halves its own whitened objective on tensors that carry three quarters of the file's KLD. At every size where held-out text was scored, it damages kinds of text its calibration does not cover. Letting GPTQ refit the grid is better than freezing it in eleven of twelve cells. (2) Whitened low-rank correction improves its base on every corpus measured, by about half as much off-distribution. Against the stock types it falls short: an I-quant type at fewer bytes beats it on every corpus at 8B and 27B; at 27B its 5% lead over the adjacent K-quant holds only on the calibration distribution; and a same-byte file mixing stock types beats it on every corpus at 27B and by a factor of three at 0.8B. (3) Against released GSQ-RCO checkpoints, KLD to the reference and likelihood of the text disagree within one run at a 2,048-token context: trained files trade fidelity for likelihood, so KLD ranks fidelity, not quality.
