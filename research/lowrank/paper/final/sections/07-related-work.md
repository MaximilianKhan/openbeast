# 7. Related work

**The correction lineage.** Adding a low-rank branch to a quantized base begins with ZeroQuant-V2's LoRC (arXiv:2303.08302), where an unweighted rank-8 SVD acts as stabilizer rather than fixer; LQER (arXiv:2402.02446) states our premise in published form — the raw quantization residual is Marchenko–Pastur-shaped, so plain-SVD capture is ≈ r/d and *weighted* decomposition is where the value lives — and QERA (arXiv:2410.06040) supplies our reference mathematics: its Theorem 1 is the full-covariance whitened closed form we use, and its Theorem 2's diagonal special case is *literally llama.cpp's imatrix statistic*, which is why our pipeline's diagonal-to-full-covariance ladder is exactly the QERA-approx→QERA-exact step measured at production scale. EoRA (arXiv:2410.21271) is the closest industry neighbor (eigenspace projection ≡ QERA-exact; ~3× full-vs-diagonal at 3-bit), and CALDERA (arXiv:2405.18886) contributes both the joint alternation-with-best-iterate template we adopt and — in its own tables — the fixed-rank benefit collapsing with scale (r64 buys 0.86 PPL at 7B; r128 buys 0.05 at 70B). What none of this lineage reports, and we add: same-pipeline diagonal-versus-full ablations on *deployed* K/I-quant formats at 0.6B–35B, comparisons against the format's own quantization ladder rather than round-to-nearest, and mixed-quant *interpolation controls* at matched bytes — the control that, at 27B and MoE scale, our own corrections lose to (§4.3).

**Rank must scale with width.** The capture decay we measure and fit in §4.2 (a fitted form over four models and one width step, not a law) has three published legs: CALDERA's Theorem 4.1 bound, the Bid-Up/OLrC lower bound (arXiv:2606.01412) forcing fixed-rank share to decay ~2r/d, and the empirical twins — LRC (arXiv:2412.07902), where r/d = 10% halves the W4A4 gap and 30% closes it; ResQ (arXiv:2412.14363), which hard-codes r = d/8 in production; FLRQ (arXiv:2601.05684), whose allocated average rank 39 beats uniform r256 at a fifth of the bytes; and TwinQuant (arXiv:2606.01556), measuring LLaMA3-8B spectra flat beyond rank 256. RILQ (arXiv:2412.01129) publishes the plateau's diagnosis — 2-bit error is inherently high-rank and layer-local SVD saturates — which our whitened 27B numbers confirm on shipped grids.

**Frozen-grid discrete refinement.** Re-choosing a quantized model's codes on its shipped grids is a small, recent family whose members are — provably — descent procedures on one layerwise quadratic (the L6 note derives each placement). GPTQ (arXiv:2210.17323) is the ancestor: its error-feedback sweep is Babai's nearest-plane algorithm, and on frozen grids Bid-Up proves per-sweep non-increase. ReQuant (arXiv:2608.07019) performs exact cyclic coordinate descent on the same objective (its −Δq·g + Δq²·H̃_jj score is the exact per-coordinate change; verified from its Eqs. 5–10), with a GPTAQ-style quantized-prefix asymmetry that reduces to our symmetric case at ΔX = 0 — generic PTQ, not GGUF. SchurQuant (arXiv:2608.15567) unfreezes the scale block: suffix-eliminating Schur conditioning (the chunk-block generalization of the Babai sweep's column conditioning) alternated with closed-form continuous scale/zero refits, worth +11.88pp at 2-bit with the objective held at GPTQ's — per-row-group format, not the K-quant superblock hierarchy. GSQ (arXiv:2604.18556) is the only other member operating on *shipped GGUF artifacts*: Gumbel-Softmax relaxation trained ~20 GPU-epochs against block-staged reconstruction, projecting back into format (Qwen3-8B Q2_K task average 50.03→56.28). Our re-rounder is the one-shot, whitened, CPU-cheap point in this family — in-format on the shipped superblock hierarchy, KLD-graded. §4.3c reports the artifact-level head-to-head against the released GSQ-RCO checkpoints, with our correction rather than our re-rounder as the one-shot arm (the re-rounder's codec does not yet cover the I-quant formats those checkpoints use); the result is a fidelity win for the one-shot arm and a capability win for the trained artifact over its own baseline. SchurQuant's own finding that "[l]ayer-wise reconstruction is a loose surrogate for the final language-model loss" (§1) joins our surrogate-versus-outcome series (§4.3b) as independent corroboration.

**Metric sophistication and its ceiling.** The whitening ladder ascends from the imatrix diagonal through the full input Gram (ours, GPTQ, QERA-exact) toward two-sided and end-loss metrics: GuidedQuant (arXiv:2505.07004) reaches the best pure-2-bit numbers in the lineage on block-diagonal end-loss Fisher *without any correction branch* — the ceiling our metric-ladder results point toward — and OBD-LLM (arXiv:2604.00821) applies the two-sided Kronecker-whitened objective to post-GPTQ residual correction, of which our objective is the L_g = I special case. We defend one-sidedness rather than apologize for it (§2.2): the tool path starts from a shipped GGUF where no gradients exist; the cheap diagonal end of output weighting is provably a no-op for frozen-grid rounding (E24's negative theorem); and their measured two-sided gain is on weight decomposition at 8B, not residual correction on K-quant grids. Their 10%-mean-diagonal dampening recipe (verified verbatim) and KronQ's HG-cancellation result (arXiv:2607.07964) are the adoptable details; ARHQ's residual-covariance whitening (arXiv:2605.00140) is the metric variant our cross-layer proposal inherits for free.

**The equivalence class, its floor, and its escape.** Where the low-rank bytes act turns out not to matter: §4.3b's pre-registered triple measurement — post-hoc correction, SRR-style pre-quant carve (arXiv:2602.02001), GlowQ-style shared bases (arXiv:2603.25385) — is byte-fair-indistinguishable at 27B despite wildly different surrogate captures. The class has a measured floor: AWSRC's seeded-Hadamard codec (arXiv:2608.23144), the nearest published byte-fair repair duel, captures exactly the random-subspace expectation of dominant whitened energy in our geometry (E31: 0.035 vs 0.983 at byte parity) — strictly below every low-rank member, tied only on the isotropic tail. And it has one measured escape: per-tensor type allocation, which is precisely the axis RCO (arXiv:2605.00649) optimizes with exact-budget dynamic programming at 8B — a paper containing zero mechanism-class comparison. The two results are complementary halves of one statement: *allocation is the only lever that moves at equal bytes, and it can be optimized exactly.* FLRQ and LQ-LoRA's ILP (arXiv:2311.12023) allocate rank and bits respectively under the same doctrine.

**Mixture-of-experts.** MiLo (arXiv:2504.02658) is our exact architecture on MoE — HQQ base plus truncated-SVD compensators with INT3 factors — and states its compensator is unfused; TileQ (arXiv:2605.09281) tiles within experts, consistent with our finding that pooled expert residuals are near-isotropic (§4.5): no shared subspace exists to amortize, and per-expert correction loses to a trivial quant-promotion control at 6.9 paired sigma. The router-guided compensator family (arXiv:2512.17073) spends FLOPs we don't have; we are byte-bound.

**Serving is part of the claim.** The field's artifacts conspicuously lack fused decode paths: EoRA ships unfused in HF/vLLM (its GPTQModel kernel is the lone exception), CALDERA's Table 6 records the decode loss, MiLo declares its compensator unfused, and SVDQuant's Nunchaku (arXiv:2411.05007) — the fusion template our kernels follow, with its measured naive +50% latency collapsing to 5–10% fused — ships low-rank-over-NVFP4 for diffusion only. Our fused-GEMV trilogy and the priced serving-tax ledger (§5) exist because a correction that decodes 20–40% slower on stock llama.cpp is not the same artifact as its bare base, and the lineage's quality tables silently assume it is.

**The upstream conversation.** llama.cpp discussion #8831 proposed an LQER-style correction in 2024 and was met with the prediction (ik_llama.cpp #15) that mature quants would not benefit; this paper is a systematic empirical answer to that thread — the skeptic was mostly right about correction versus the ladder, and wrong about the grid itself, whose frozen codes carry a free 13–34% of KL divergence (§3). Two of our campaign's independent findings (the MTP-imatrix blind spot; the KV-backward seam) were already reported upstream (#23476/#23575; #21037) and are treated here as confirmations, per our stated novelty protocol (§2.5).

**Measurement stance.** We grade artifacts by mean KL divergence against reference logits with paired per-chunk statistics — KLD because it tracks answer flips at Spearman 0.981 (arXiv:2407.09141) and is the deployment community's working currency (llama.cpp #4110) — with perplexity secondary under a stated silent-zone caveat (arXiv:2606.19558). Our own §4.3c is a counterexample to leaning on it across refinement families, and we report it as one. The same-harness survey's warning that cross-paper numbers are incomparable without shared calibration and evaluation is one this paper takes as a design constraint rather than a disclaimer: every comparison inside is same-pipeline, same-tokens, same-reference.
