# 8. Related work

**Low-rank correction of quantized weights.** Adding a low-rank branch to a quantized base begins with LoRC in ZeroQuant-V2 (arXiv:2303.08302), an unweighted rank-8 SVD. LQER (arXiv:2402.02446) observes that the raw residual has a Marchenko-Pastur spectrum, so the value lies in a weighted decomposition. QERA (arXiv:2410.06040) supplies the mathematics we use: its Theorem 1 is the full-covariance whitened closed form, and the diagonal special case in its Theorem 2 is the statistic llama.cpp's imatrix computes. EoRA (arXiv:2410.21271) is the nearest industrial counterpart, and CALDERA (arXiv:2405.18886) contributes the alternation with best-iterate tracking and reports, in its own tables, the fixed-rank benefit shrinking with model size. What this paper adds to that line is the setting: the same pipeline measured with diagonal and full whitening on llama.cpp's deployed K-quant and I-quant formats at 0.6B to 35B, compared against the format's own ladder and, at 27B and on a mixture-of-experts model, against a mixed-type control at matched bytes, which the correction loses to (§4.3, §4.6).

**Rank and width.** The decay we fit in §4.2 has published counterparts: CALDERA's Theorem 4.1; the lower bound of arXiv:2606.01412; LRC (arXiv:2412.07902), where rank at 10% of width halves the W4A4 gap; ResQ (arXiv:2412.14363), which fixes rank at an eighth of width; FLRQ (arXiv:2601.05684), whose allocated ranks beat a uniform rank at fewer bytes; and TwinQuant (arXiv:2606.01556), which reports flat spectra beyond rank 256. RILQ (arXiv:2412.01129) reports that 2-bit error is high-rank and that layer-local correction saturates.

**Re-selecting codes on a fixed grid.** GPTQ (arXiv:2210.17323) is the source of our sweep. ReQuant (arXiv:2608.07019) performs cyclic coordinate descent on the same quadratic for generic post-training quantization, not GGUF. SchurQuant (arXiv:2608.15567) also refits the scales, and reports that layer-wise reconstruction is a loose surrogate for the final loss, which agrees with §4.7. GSQ (arXiv:2604.18556) is the other method released as GGUF files: Gumbel-Softmax relaxation trained against block-staged reconstruction and projected back into the format. Our re-rounder is a one-shot, CPU-only member of this group that works on the K-quant block hierarchy. §5 compares released GSQ-RCO checkpoints with our correction, not with our re-rounder, whose codec does not cover I-quants. Our placement of these methods relative to one objective is argued in a project note [source: research/lowrank/paper/theory-L6-family-subsumption.md].

Three neighbouring classes of work are not compared in this paper and are not cited with numbers: learned or adaptive rounding on a fixed grid, which is the older form of "re-choose only the codes"; vector and lattice quantizers at 2 bits, which are strong baselines outside the GGUF format; and Hessian-aware mixed-precision bit allocation, which is the systematic version of the type-allocation control we built by hand. §7.1 lists these as limitations.

**Richer metrics.** GuidedQuant (arXiv:2505.07004) reaches strong 2-bit results with a block-diagonal end-loss Fisher and no correction branch. OBD-LLM (arXiv:2604.00821) applies a two-sided Kronecker-whitened objective to residual correction after GPTQ; ours is its one-sided case. We stay one-sided for the reasons given in §2.2. KronQ (arXiv:2607.07964) and ARHQ (arXiv:2605.00140) are further variants of the metric that we did not test.

**Placement and allocation.** §4.4 compares post-hoc correction with a pre-quantization carve in the order of SRR (arXiv:2602.02001) and with shared bases in the arrangement of GlowQ (arXiv:2603.25385), and could not distinguish them within its bound. AWSRC (arXiv:2608.23144) uses seeded bases; in our whitened geometry such bases capture what a random subspace would (§4.4). Per-tensor type allocation is what RCO (arXiv:2605.00649) optimizes with an exact-budget dynamic program; FLRQ and the integer program of LQ-LoRA (arXiv:2311.12023) allocate rank and bits in the same spirit.

**Mixture-of-experts.** MiLo (arXiv:2504.02658) uses our architecture on such models, a quantized base with low-rank compensators, and states that its compensator is unfused. TileQ (arXiv:2605.09281) tiles within experts, consistent with our finding that pooled expert residuals have no shared subspace (§4.6). The router-guided compensators of arXiv:2512.17073 spend computation that a byte-bound setting does not have.

**Serving.** Few released correction methods ship a fused decode path: EoRA ships unfused in HF/vLLM, CALDERA's Table 6 records the decode loss, MiLo's compensator is unfused, and SVDQuant (arXiv:2411.05007), whose fusion design our kernels follow, ships for diffusion models. §6 measures the cost on llama.cpp because a correction that decodes 20-40% slower than its bare base is a different artifact from that base.

**The upstream discussion.** llama.cpp discussion #8831 proposed an LQER-style correction in 2024 and was met with the prediction (ik_llama.cpp #15) that mature quantizers would not benefit. Our measurements agree with that prediction for correction against the ladder. Two observations made during this work, about the imatrix on multi-token-prediction layers and about backpropagation through the KV cache, had already been reported upstream (#23476, #23575; #21037) and are not claimed as new.

**Measurement.** We score files by mean KL divergence against reference logits with paired per-chunk statistics, following the observation that KL divergence tracks answer flips (arXiv:2407.09141) and llama.cpp practice (#4110), with perplexity secondary (arXiv:2606.19558). §5 contains one pair in which that measure and a task suite disagree, and we report it as a limit on the measure.
