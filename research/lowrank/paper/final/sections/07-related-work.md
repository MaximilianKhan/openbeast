# 8. Related work

**Low-rank correction of quantized weights.** Adding a low-rank branch to a quantized base begins with LoRC in ZeroQuant-V2 (arXiv:2303.08302), an unweighted rank-8 SVD. LQER (arXiv:2402.02446) observes that the raw residual has a Marchenko-Pastur spectrum, so the value lies in a weighted decomposition. QERA (arXiv:2410.06040) supplies the mathematics we use: its Theorem 1 is the full-covariance whitened closed form, and the diagonal special case in its Theorem 2 is the statistic llama.cpp's imatrix computes. EoRA (arXiv:2410.21271) is the nearest industrial counterpart, and CALDERA (arXiv:2405.18886) contributes the alternation with best-iterate tracking and reports, in its own tables, the fixed-rank benefit shrinking with model size. What this paper adds to that line is the setting: the same pipeline measured with diagonal and full whitening on llama.cpp's deployed K-quant and I-quant formats at 0.6B to 35B, compared against the format's own ladder and against mixed-type controls at matched bytes at 0.8B, at 27B and on a mixture-of-experts model, which the correction loses to (§4.3, §4.6), on and off the calibration distribution. The newest member of the lineage, a two-stage compensator with an asymmetric, output-Fisher-weighted target (arXiv:2610.08164), argues that the symmetric target the lineage solves is itself high-rank and recovers much more of the held-out gap on a 2-bit base; it builds no same-byte control, and our verdicts are scoped to the symmetric kind (§4.3).

**Rank and width.** The decay we fit in §4.2 has published counterparts: CALDERA's Theorem 4.1; the lower bound of arXiv:2606.01412; LRC (arXiv:2412.07902), where rank at 10% of width halves the W4A4 gap; ResQ (arXiv:2412.14363), which fixes rank at an eighth of width; FLRQ (arXiv:2601.05684), whose allocated ranks beat a uniform rank at fewer bytes; and TwinQuant (arXiv:2606.01556), which reports flat spectra beyond rank 256. RILQ (arXiv:2412.01129) reports that 2-bit error is high-rank and that layer-local correction saturates.

**Re-selecting codes on a fixed grid.** GPTQ (arXiv:2210.17323) is the source of our sweep. ReQuant (arXiv:2608.07019) performs cyclic coordinate descent on the same quadratic for generic post-training quantization, not GGUF. DRQ (arXiv:2610.11226) refines codes on a frozen grid, reports that lower calibration reconstruction loss raises held-out perplexity, and repairs it with a worst-case objective over activation distributions; SARQC (arXiv:2605.05693) penalizes drift from the original weights for the same reason; JARQ (arXiv:2609.38599) shows that refitting the grid to the codes beats refining codes or scales alone. Inside the GGUF formats, AutoRound exports gradient-tuned K-quant files (arXiv:2309.05516), and IST-DASLab's gptq-gguf-toolkit (2025) runs GPTQ with the K-quant grid refit at each superblock, the free-grid arm of our §3.4. SchurQuant (arXiv:2608.15567) also refits the scales, and reports that layer-wise reconstruction is a loose surrogate for the final loss, which agrees with §4.7. GSQ (arXiv:2604.18556) is the other method released as GGUF files: Gumbel-Softmax relaxation trained against block-staged reconstruction and projected back into the format. Our re-rounder is a one-shot, CPU-only member of this group that works on the K-quant block hierarchy; §3.4 compares it with GPTQ allowed to refit the grid inside the same format. §5 compares released GSQ-RCO checkpoints with our correction, not with our re-rounder, whose codec does not cover I-quants. Our placement of these methods relative to one objective is argued in a project note [source: research/lowrank/paper/theory-L6-family-subsumption.md].

Three neighbouring classes of work are not compared in this paper: learned or adaptive rounding on a fixed grid, which is the older form of "re-choose only the codes"; quantizers that add incoherence processing, trained codebooks or trellis codes to vector quantization (QuIP#, AQLM, QTIP), where llama.cpp's I-quants are the lattice-codebook member without incoherence processing; and systematic mixed-precision allocation, the version of the type-allocation control we built by hand. §7.1 lists these as limitations.

**Richer metrics.** GuidedQuant (arXiv:2505.07004) reaches strong 2-bit results with a block-diagonal end-loss Fisher and no correction branch. OBD-LLM (arXiv:2604.00821) applies a two-sided Kronecker-whitened objective to residual correction after GPTQ; ours is its one-sided case. We stay one-sided for the reasons given in §2.2. KronQ (arXiv:2607.07964) and ARHQ (arXiv:2605.00140) are further variants of the metric that we did not test.

**Placement and allocation.** §4.4 compares post-hoc correction with a pre-quantization carve in the order of SRR (arXiv:2602.02001) and with shared bases in the arrangement of GlowQ (arXiv:2603.25385), and could not distinguish them within its bound. AWSRC (arXiv:2608.23144) uses seeded bases; in our whitened geometry such bases capture what a random subspace would (§4.4). Per-tensor type allocation is what RCO (arXiv:2605.00649) optimizes with an exact-budget dynamic program, and EvoPress (arXiv:2410.14649) by evolutionary search; on Qwen3.8-27B a probe-based allocation of llama.cpp K-quant types beats the stock IQ3_M mix at near-equal bytes, and the same work finds round-to-nearest error spectrally flat (arXiv:2609.33923). At a matched budget, spending bits globally beats repairing the most recoverable layers locally (arXiv:2609.01587), which is the allocation-side counterpart of our control result. FLRQ and the integer program of LQ-LoRA (arXiv:2311.12023) allocate rank and bits in the same spirit.

**Mixture-of-experts.** MiLo (arXiv:2504.02658) uses our architecture on such models, a quantized base with low-rank compensators, and states that its compensator is unfused. TileQ (arXiv:2605.09281) tiles within experts, consistent with our finding that pooled expert residuals have no shared subspace (§4.6). The router-guided compensators of arXiv:2512.17073 spend computation that a byte-bound setting does not have.

**Serving.** Fused decode paths for correction branches now exist for GPU 4-bit kernels: SPEAR fuses a gated low-rank compensator into the GEMM epilogue at batch 1 (+25% over the bare kernel at 1B, +6-14% at 70B; arXiv:2606.11244), and TwinQuant and FLRQ report 4-8% overhead; DecDEC fuses a non-low-rank residual at 1.7% on a laptop GPU (arXiv:2412.20185); SVDQuant (arXiv:2411.05007), whose fusion design our kernels follow, ships for diffusion models. EoRA ships unfused in HF/vLLM, CALDERA's Table 6 records the decode loss, and MiLo's compensator is unfused. None targets llama.cpp's K-quant and I-quant formats, where the stock adapter path has no fused decode (an open upstream issue, #27093, reports about 27% token-generation loss from a rank-1 adapter). §6 measures the cost on llama.cpp because a correction that decodes 20-40% slower than its bare base is a different artifact from that base.

**The upstream discussion.** llama.cpp discussion #8831 proposed an LQER-style correction in 2024 and was met with the prediction (ik_llama.cpp #15) that mature quantizers would not benefit. That thread also posed the same-byte question in so many words, showed a 4.30-bpw L²QER result trailing IQ4_XS at 4.25 bpw, and reported an informal test on a 270M model in which a carve plus correction was no better than the stock Q2_K mix. Our measurements are the controlled version of that test, and agree with it for correction against the ladder. Two observations made during this work, about the imatrix on multi-token-prediction layers and about backpropagation through the KV cache, had already been reported upstream (#23476, #23575; #21037) and are not claimed as new.

**Measurement.** We score files by mean KL divergence against reference logits with paired per-chunk statistics, following the observation that KL divergence tracks answer flips (arXiv:2407.09141) and llama.cpp practice (#4110), with perplexity secondary. Total variation tracks flips more closely than KL divergence across models and corpora (arXiv:2610.00694). That KL divergence measures displacement from the reference and not its direction is argued for GGUF quants in arXiv:2606.19558, and for trained against distilled files in arXiv:2601.20088; §5.4 gives a paired, matched-byte instance in which KL divergence and likelihood rank the same released files oppositely, and we report it as a limit on the measure.
