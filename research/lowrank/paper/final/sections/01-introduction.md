# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 The setting

A consumer GPU such as the RTX 5090 pairs a fast processor with 32 GB of memory, and the models people want to run on it are several times larger than that at training precision. Single-stream decoding is limited by memory bandwidth, because every token reads the full weight set. We measured the consequence on one 27B model, Qwen3.6-27B (the checkpoint is heretic-v2, an uncensored community finetune of Qwen3.6-27B that keeps the base model's multi-token-prediction layers): the Q6_K file occupies 23.6 GB of VRAM and decodes at 61 tok/s, and the Q2_K file occupies 13.0 GB and decodes at 99.7 tok/s [source: research/lowrank/RESULTS_ROLLUP.md]. On this card, for these two files, the smaller one was also the faster one. We take that as the motivation for the study and not as a general result.

The question is how much quality post-training processing can recover per byte, or at zero added bytes, on top of a mature quantization stack. We ask it on llama.cpp, whose family of imatrix-weighted block quantizers (the K-quant and I-quant types, nominally about 1.5 to 6.5 bits per weight; terms are defined in §2) is the strongest deployed baseline in this regime and a harder rival than round-to-nearest. The models are Qwen-family [PENDING:G — plus Llama-3.1-8B], all measurements come from one GPU, and every comparison is scored on the calibration distribution and on two corpora that no calibration touched.

## 1.2 What this paper does not claim

It does not claim that low-rank factorization compresses these models. A singular-value census of every tensor in our smallest model finds that capturing 95% of unweighted Frobenius energy needs 50-80% of full rank on every projection type [source: research/lowrank/experiments/01-svd-spectrum/README.md]. The quantization residual R = W − dequant(Q), the object that the error-correction lineage corrects (ZeroQuant-V2/LoRC, arXiv:2303.08302; LQER, arXiv:2402.02446; QERA, arXiv:2410.06040; EoRA, arXiv:2410.21271; CALDERA, arXiv:2405.18886), is less structured still: 90% of its energy needs about 62% of full rank [source: research/lowrank/experiments/03-residual-rank/README.md]. Both observations agree with the published account (LQER; RILQ, arXiv:2412.01129).

It does not claim a method that beats llama.cpp's stock quantization types. At 27B the corrected configuration is ahead of Q3_K_S by 0.0047 KLD (5%, t = −2.62) on the calibration distribution, behind it on FineWeb-Edu (t = +3.26) and unresolved on code; a same-byte file that mixes stock types and has no adapter is ahead of it on all three corpora, and the I-quant type IQ3_XS is better than both at fewer bytes (§4.3). At 0.8B the same-byte mixed-type control has a third of the corrected configuration's KLD [source: research/lowrank/experiments/36-submission/RESULTS-A.md; experiments/36-submission/RESULTS-D.md].

## 1.3 Contributions

**(1) Frozen-grid re-rounding, and the calibration it needs.** llama-quantize chooses a grid per block and then rounds each element independently. We keep the grid bytes as shipped and re-choose only the codes under a captured activation-covariance metric, using the GPTQ sweep (arXiv:2210.17323). The output is a standard GGUF of identical size. On Qwen3.5-0.8B at Q2_K it lowers KL divergence by 33% against the same file before re-rounding (t = −28.4, 40/40 chunks) [PENDING:B — 27B single-step result] [source: research/lowrank/experiments/36-submission/RESULTS-D.md]. A grid of five calibration texts against three evaluation corpora shows where the gain lands: on the kinds of text the calibration covers. Calibrated on prose, the file is worse on code than before re-rounding, and the reverse; a calibration mixing prose and code improves all three corpora, and calibration-sampling variance is small against these effects (§3.1). Re-rounding improves a given file and does not make Q2_K the best choice at its size: the smaller stock IQ3_XXS file has lower KL divergence on all three corpora. [PENDING:E — free-grid GPTQ baseline at identical format and bytes (§3.4).] The tool needs the higher-precision source weights and a covariance capture made with a patched binary; it does not operate on a GGUF alone. Related methods are GSQ (arXiv:2604.18556), ReQuant (arXiv:2608.07019) and SchurQuant (arXiv:2608.15567); the relation is stated in §8.

**(2) A paired audit of low-rank correction on llama.cpp's own formats, on and off the calibration distribution.** We measure whitened low-rank correction at three dense sizes and on one mixture-of-experts model, against the stock types and against mixed-type controls built at the same bytes at 0.8B, at 27B and on the mixture-of-experts model. The correction improves its own base everywhere we measured it, with a recovered fraction that grows as the base gets thinner (7%, 12% and 32% at Q2_K, IQ2_XS and IQ1_S, with the recipe fixed) and halves off-distribution (§4.5). It loses to the control everywhere one was built. Three placements of the low-rank bytes cannot be told apart at 27B, within a bound we state (§4.4). Fixed-rank capture is sublinear in rank and, across a single width step, falls more slowly than r/d predicts (§4.2). Several of the audit's negative results are its best-resolved findings (§4.7, Appendix A).

**(3) A head-to-head with released trained checkpoints, and what KL divergence measures.** Against the released GSQ-RCO checkpoints of Qwen3.8-27B, the untrained vendor baseline at matched bytes is closer to the BF16 distribution, at 512 and at 2,048 tokens, while the trained checkpoints assign the text higher likelihood in the same 2,048-token run. KL divergence to a reference ranks fidelity to that reference; it is the right instrument for a method that only approximates the reference and the wrong one for ranking a trained artifact against an untrained one (§5.4). Our correction (+0.9 GB, not byte-matched) moves the baseline closer to the reference on every corpus and context we measured, and a stock file at near-equal total bytes beats it. [PENDING:F — capability replication, three runs per arm.]

**(4) Serving kernels.** llama.cpp's stock adapter path costs 20-40% of decode speed. A fused-kernel series removes most of that at 0.6B (+79%, one same-session pair) and gives +2.7% at 27B (N = 10, interleaved). A separate allocator fix raised base decode at 0.6B by 19.6% in the better of two sessions and 11% in the other, with no measurable change at 27B (§6) [source: research/lowrank/experiments/14-fused-kernel/REPORT-PHASE3.md, experiments/25-alloc-concurrency/REPORT.md].

The measurement protocol these results follow (single-step provenance, paired per-chunk statistics, held-out corpora, same-byte controls, pre-registration) is part of the method and is stated in §2.5.

## 1.4 Relation to prior work

The pipeline is assembled from published mathematics: QERA's whitened closed forms, LoftQ's alternation, and GPTQ's sweep on a frozen grid. What we add is where it is measured: on llama.cpp's deployed formats, against its stock types and a same-byte mixed-type control, with paired statistics. §8 gives the comparison in full.

The paper follows the pipeline: method (§2), re-rounding (§3), the correction audit (§4), the GSQ-RCO head-to-head (§5), serving (§6), limitations (§7), related work (§8). Appendix A holds additional negative results.
