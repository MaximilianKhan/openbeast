## Part 1 — Referee report

### 1.1 Summary of claims

1. **Re-rounding** (GPTQ sweep on llama-quantize's frozen Q2_K grid, zero added bytes) lowers KLD by
   33% at 0.8B, gives no resolved gain on the calibration distribution at 8B and 27B, and damages
   held-out text at every size where held-out text was scored. The calibration grid at 0.8B shows the
   gain lands on the kinds of text the calibration covers. Refitting the grid (free-grid GPTQ) beats the
   frozen grid in 11/12 cells. Mechanism at 27B: the sweep halves its whitened objective while the
   unweighted error rises, so it "optimizes its proxy and not the outcome".
2. **Low-rank correction** improves its base everywhere, "by about half as much off-distribution", with
   recovery rising as the base thins (7/12/32%). Against stock types it loses: IQ3_XS at fewer bytes beats
   it at 8B and 27B; its 5% lead over Q3_K_S at 27B holds only on wikitext; a same-byte mixed-type control
   beats it on every corpus at 27B and ~3× at 0.8B. On Llama-3.1-8B it beats Q3_K_S on both prose corpora.
3. **GSQ-RCO head-to-head**: at 2,048 tokens KLD and NLL disagree in one run; "trained files trade fidelity
   for likelihood, so KLD ranks fidelity, not quality".
4. **Kernels**: +79% decode at 0.6B, +2.7% at 27B.

### 1.2 Strengths

- The revision ran essentially everything the previous review asked for, under a plan committed to git
  before the scoring runs (a2b4d25, 16:10; first E36 score 16:16), and it reports the failures it
  registered: B1 and B3 fail, the 27B ladder margin reverses off-distribution, the 0.8B control dominates.
  This is unusually honest work.
- The E36 numbers are almost all correct. Of 230+ values checked, every table KLD, ± sem, paired mean,
  t, win count, byte count and GB conversion in §3.1, §3.4, §4.3, §4.5, §5.4 and A.3 matched its source
  (Part 2). The block-split analysis of the 27B wiki margin, the pre-fix margins and the 0.87 run-to-run
  correlation all reproduce from the raw logs.
- Same-byte controls, held-out corpora, single-step provenance, a non-Qwen model, and a free-grid baseline
  at identical format and bytes are exactly the right instruments, and they changed conclusions.
- §7.6 (corrections to the record) and §4.7 (surrogate-vs-outcome table) are models of disclosure.

### 1.3 Major weaknesses (ranked)

**M1. "About half as much off-distribution" is contradicted by the paper's own data.**
Abstract: "Whitened low-rank correction improves its base on every corpus measured, by about half as
much off-distribution." §7.5: "about half as much off the calibration distribution". The halving holds
only for Qwen3.6-27B (§4.3: 0.0108 → 0.0045/0.0051; §4.5: 7.0 → 3.3%, 12.2 → 7.6%, 32.0 → 16.3%).
Elsewhere:
- §5.3 itself: "a correction whitened on wikitext lowers its base on FineWeb-Edu by nearly the same
  amount" (−0.0055 → −0.0052; −0.0021 → −0.0022; Qwen3.8-27B).
- 0.8B (paired-d.txt, fc-wiki vs Q2_K): wiki −0.322, FineWeb −0.310 (96% of the wiki increment; 58% vs
  66% relative recovery).
- 8B (RESULTS-G): wiki −0.0221, FineWeb −0.0153 (69%).
Fix: scope the halving to Qwen3.6-27B and say the off-distribution increment ranges from about half to
about the same at the other models. Same in §1.3 (2) and §4.8 ("a halving of the increment at 27B" is
already correctly scoped there).

**M2. The stated mechanism for the 27B re-rounding failure is not supported by the check offered.**
§3.1 heading: "Why it fails at size: the sweep optimizes its proxy and not the outcome", then: "The sweep
moves error out of the directions the calibration activations occupy and into the others, and text whose
activations occupy other directions sees more error than before." Abstract and §1.3 generalize: "halves
its own whitened objective on tensors that carry three quarters of the file's KLD"; "the unweighted error
of those tensors rising 16-25%".
Problems:
1. `checks/rr27b-independent-dequant.txt` covers **3 of 256** swept tensors (ratios 0.487/0.569/0.629;
   one tensor each of attn_k, ffn_up, ssm_out). No script is saved beside it ("own code"), so it cannot be
   rerun. "Halves" for the file and "those tensors" in §1.3 rest on n = 3; the sweep log
   (`logs-b/reround-wiki.log`) prints no objective values, so "as the sweep reports" is unverifiable.
2. The mechanism explains held-out damage but **not the on-distribution failure**: if error moved out of
   the wiki-activation subspace and the wiki whitened error halved, wikitext should improve; it moved
   −3.7% (t = −1.87) and wiki **NLL got worse** (ΔNLL +0.021, t = +3.71, RESULTS-B, not reported).
   Something other than "directions not covered" is needed, e.g. in-sample overfit of a poorly estimated
   Gram (48 × 512 = 24,576 tokens for 5,120-dim inputs, ≈ 5 samples per dimension at 27B and ≈ 6 at 8B,
   against ≈ 20 at 0.8B — which would also explain the size dependence), or layer-local vs end-to-end
   error propagation.
3. The direct test of the stated mechanism was not run: whitened error under a **held-out** Gram (a
   wiki.test Gram for the in-distribution question; the existing `gram27b-e36-mixed` or a FineWeb/code
   Gram for the off-distribution question). That is a CPU-only computation on files already on disk.
Fix: retitle as an observation ("the proxy improves, the outcome does not"), state n = 3, drop or label
as hypothesis the directional mechanism, mention the wiki NLL regression, and either run the held-out-Gram
check or list the sample-to-dimension ratio among the untested candidates in the "What separates" paragraph.
Also make the abstract's "halves its own whitened objective" apply to 27B only (no 8B check or oracle exists).

**M3. Contribution (3) generalizes from one method, one corpus and one marginal t.**
Abstract: "trained files trade fidelity for likelihood, so KLD ranks fidelity, not quality." §5.6: "the
wrong one for ranking a trained file against an untrained one." Evidence: two GSQ-RCO files, wikitext
only, 40 windows; the NLL advantage is resolved at IQ3 only (t = −2.49, unadjusted) and not at IQ2
(t = −1.64). On FineWeb-Edu (E32, 512 tokens; recomputed here) GSQ-RCO and its baseline are **tied in
NLL** (t = 0.00 and −0.91), and no FineWeb 2,048-token run exists. The calibration corpus of the released
files is undocumented (§5.3), so a wikitext-specific likelihood gain is also consistent with
training on wikitext-like text — that is not "quality". NLL of one public corpus is not quality either.
Fix: "on wikitext at 2,048 tokens, the two released GSQ-RCO files have higher KLD to BF16 and lower NLL
(resolved at IQ3) than their baselines; on FineWeb-Edu at 512 tokens their NLL is level." Drop
"trained files" (generic) and "quality" until F lands.

**M4. NLL is used selectively.** The paper elevates NLL to "likelihood of the text" in §5.4 but never
reports it where it cuts the other way. Computed from the existing logs:
- Our correction at 2,048 tokens on UD-IQ3_S: ΔNLL −0.0003, t = −0.22 (RESULTS-C); at 512 tokens t = −0.31
  (wiki) and −0.94 (FineWeb). The correction improves KLD, not likelihood, at IQ3.
- Ours-IQ3 vs GSQ-RCO IQ3 at 2,048: GSQ-RCO has lower NLL (ΔNLL +0.0177, t = +2.43) despite 1.17 GB fewer
  bytes. §5.4 reports only that "our correction ... keeps its increment at 2048 tokens".
- 27B wiki: IQ3_XS vs corrected ΔNLL +0.0139, t = +2.42 — the corrected configuration has lower NLL than
  IQ3_XS on the calibration corpus, while the text says IQ3_XS "is better than both".
- 27B rr-wiki vs Q2_K: wiki NLL worse, t = +3.71 (would strengthen §3.1).
- T1.9 Q2_K + fc vs Q2_K: wiki ΔNLL t = −1.27 (unresolved).
Fix: one NLL column (or sentence) for every load-bearing KLD pair in §3.1, §4.3, §4.5, §5.4, or an
explicit statement that NLL is reported only in §5.4 and why.

**M5. Drift from registered rules.**
1. PLAN §0: "the Bonferroni-adjusted p is reported beside each contested pair." No RESULTS file and no
   paper table reports one. The contested C1 wiki margin (p = 0.010) does not survive a factor of 6
   (the A-pairs per corpus: 0.06) or 18 (0.18).
2. PLAN §B3: "the paper's 27B headline becomes the rr-mixed row whichever arm looks better afterwards."
   The abstract and §1.3 lead with the wiki arm ("no resolved gain on the calibration distribution and
   harms both held-out corpora"). The registered headline is worse: the deployable recipe makes the file
   **worse** on wikitext (t = +3.21) and FineWeb (+13.25), level on code. Say that in the abstract.
3. PLAN §D T1.15 asked for SD and range of RR's KLD **and PPL**; the paper gives the ΔKLD range only.
4. PLAN in-text timestamps are later than the git commits that contain them (§B–§G "16:25–16:50" in a
   16:10 commit; 8B amendment "17:30" in a 16:42 commit; 0.8B amendment "18:10" in a 17:00 commit; B6
   "20:55" in a 20:35 commit). The commits precede every outcome, so registration holds, but the in-text
   times are wrong. B5 "registered 20:40 … before running": the oracle GGUF build started 20:33:14 and
   finished 20:34:41, before both 20:40 and the commit (20:34:54); its scoring ran at 02:25 next day,
   so no outcome was seen. §2.5 "committed to version control before any of them ran": E36-A reference
   generation started 16:04, six minutes before the commit (no outcome either). Fix: cite commit hashes and
   times, not in-text times, and say "before any scoring".

**M6. The one positive 27B result for the correction is weaker than the abstract makes it.**
Abstract: "at 27B its 5% lead over the adjacent K-quant holds only on the calibration distribution" reads as
an established on-distribution lead. It is: p = 0.010 unadjusted; zero on chunks 1-40 (+0.0004, t = +0.13),
all on chunks 41-100 (recomputed: −0.0081, t = −3.99); a re-scoring of the same 100 chunks whose size was
reached by sequential looks at 20 and 40 on the pre-fix build. §4.3 discloses all of this, which is good,
but the abstract and §7.5 do not qualify it. Fix: "a marginal 5% lead on wikitext (p = 0.01, not
corrected for multiplicity, confined to 60 of the 100 chunks)". Cheapest real fix: score the whole
wikitext-2 test set (≈ 580 chunks). Each 100-chunk 27B score took about 20 s (run-a.log) and a 100-chunk
reference about 4 min, so this is well under an hour of GPU and removes the prefix-selection problem.

**M7. "No resolved gain" is a threshold artifact that hides a small, probably real gain — and one arm that passes.**
B1 fails at t = −1.87 (27B) and −1.97 (8B), but the bootstrap CIs exclude zero ([−0.0124, −0.0002];
[−0.0105, −0.0001]), Wilcoxon p = 0.048 at 27B, sign p = 0.035 at 8B. The B6 arm (same base, pre-fix BF16
Grams) is **resolved better than the file as written on wikitext** (−0.0080, t = −2.87, Wilcoxon 0.0017,
block bootstrap excludes 0). §3.1 quotes only "−0.0080 against the file as written, but level with the
current-Gram arm, t = −1.11", without its t against the bare file, under the sentence "neither do the pre-fix
BF16 Grams … reproduce it". The abstract states "at 8B and 27B it gives no resolved gain on the calibration
distribution". Fix: "a 2-5% gain at the edge of resolution (t = −1.87, −1.97; one Gram capture reaches
t = −2.87)", which leaves the robust finding — held-out damage — unchanged.

**M8. B6's "provenance" reading is inference by elimination, and one alternative was never removed.**
§3.1/RESULTS-B: the gap to the legacy −13.3% is "provenance (legacy source and Grams were Q6_K-derived)".
The legacy *bare* Q2_K was never re-scored on b10865 (only the legacy re-rounded file was, as a continuity
row), so the legacy pair's gain on the current build is unknown. The legacy files also differ in recipe
and bytes (legacy re-rounded file 10,864,595,360 B vs single-step Q2_K 11,003,736,448 B), so "provenance"
conflates source precision with tensor-type mix. Also unreported: re-scored on the current build, the
legacy re-rounded file is worse than the single-step Q2_K on FineWeb (t = +7.93) and code (t = +12.53), so
the legacy artifact showed the same held-out damage. Fix: re-score the legacy bare file (if it is still on
disk, minutes of GPU) or state the inference as untested; report the legacy held-out rows.

- m3. **Calibration grid wording** (§3.1): "Both calibrations that contain both kinds improve all three

- m6. **Pre-/post-fix provenance of the artifacts is not stated.** All 27B bases (including IQ2_M, IQ2_XS,
  IQ1_S, the control) were quantized with the pre-fix E27 imatrix (`gram27b-bf16`, 2026-08-04); the §4.3

  Scoring is all on b10865. Within-table comparisons are fair, but §2.5 ("Every row … is scored on b10865")

- m7. **8B wording.** §4.3: "On Llama, then, a calibrated correction beats the adjacent K-quant type
  off-distribution as well as on it" — on code it is level (t = +1.44). The 8B control result (correction
  beats it on both prose corpora) is absent from the abstract, which reports controls only where they win.
  The 8B "control" is Q3_K_S + 11.7 MB; disclosed, but §1.3 lists 8B among "mixed-type controls built at
  the same bytes" without the qualifier. The code-corpus C2 "behind it on code (t = +2.49)" has Wilcoxon

- m8. **§3.4 understatement**: "not better on wikitext (+2.87, −0.19)": at 8B the free-grid file is
  resolved worse (t = +2.87).
- m9. **Kernel numbers tagged [E36]** (abstract, item 4). E36 contains no speed runs; the sources are E14
  (+79%, one same-session pair) and E27 (+2.7%, N = 10). The abstract should also say "one pair" for 79%.
- m10. **A.3 mechanism.** "If the harm came from counting importance twice … it would vanish without the

- m11. **Recovery curve confound** (§4.5): "thinner" is confounded with quantizer family (K-quant Q2_K vs

- m12. **"carry 76-80% of the file's divergence"** (§3.1, B5): the oracle promotes most of the model's

- m13. **n = 40 at 0.8B** with a 4-block bootstrap (df 3) is weak; 0.8B scoring costs seconds. Raise to the

- m15. **"Kinds of text"** (abstract, §3.1) is defined after the fact: at 0.8B FineWeb counts as the same

- m16. paper.md/PDF lag `sections/06` by one paragraph (§7.4 "Re-rounding at size"); rebuild.
- m17. §2.5 "Every row of §3.1 … is scored on llama.cpp b10865" — the 0.6B rows of §3.1 are legacy.

### 1.6 Statistics, in one place

- Multiplicity: E36 alone produces well over 150 KLD contrasts; the paper says margins near |t| = 2-3 are
  suggestive (§2.5), but then makes three such margins load-bearing in the abstract (C1 wiki t = −2.62,
  GSQ NLL t = −2.49, and B1's |t| < 2 as "no gain"). Treat all three consistently.
- Dependence: the robust set is computed (lag-1 r up to +0.40); fine at n = 100, weak at n = 40 (m13).
- Prefix samples: every corpus is a prefix (first 100 wiki chunks, first 400 FineWeb docs, first 40 windows
  of the 145 at 2,048 tokens). The 1-40/41-100 heterogeneity shows prefix choice matters at the contested
  margins (M6).
- Calibration-sampling variance: measured for RR at 0.8B only (range 0.004/0.018/0.007 ΔKLD); not for the
  correction and not at 27B, where the contested margins live. Stated in §7.1; fine.
- Per-chunk recovery from 5-decimal running means adds noise of order k × 1e-5 (≤ ~1e-3 at chunk 100) to
  each chunk; small against the per-chunk SD (~0.018) and it cancels in the mean. No action needed.

### 1.7 Verdict: **Major revision** (text-scope; at most about an hour of GPU and one CPU check)

The measurements are sound and almost every E36 number is reproduced exactly. What fails TMLR's test
("are the claims supported by evidence?") is the abstract and summary. Three of the four headline claims
need to be narrowed: the off-distribution halving (M1, contradicted in the paper's own §5.3 and by the 0.8B
and 8B data), the re-rounding mechanism (M2, n = 3 tensors, and the mechanism does not explain the
on-distribution failure), and "KLD ranks fidelity, not quality" (M3, one method, one corpus, t = −2.49).
NLL is reported only where it supports the narrative (M4), and two registered commitments were not kept
(M5). None of this needs a new campaign. The requests that do need compute are cheap and optional: score
the full wikitext test set at 27B (M6), compute held-out-Gram whitened error on the three checked tensors
(M2, CPU only), and re-score the legacy bare Q2_K (M8). With those edits this is an accept-level negative-
results paper.
